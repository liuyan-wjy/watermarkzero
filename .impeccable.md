## Design Context

### Users

The primary users are Gemini image creators, indie makers, designers, and marketers who need to remove the visible Gemini watermark quickly without uploading private assets to a remote server. They typically arrive with a high-intent task, want immediate proof that the tool works, and care more about speed, trust, and clarity than novelty for its own sake.

### Brand Personality

Current working personality: **precise, trustworthy, efficient**.

The interface should feel technically credible and calm rather than flashy. The emotional goals are confidence and control: users should feel that the tool is specific, honest about limitations, and fast enough to trust for real work.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light-first “precision tool” aesthetic.

- clean, bright canvas with strong contrast
- sharp geometry and subtle grid / pixel-repair motifs
- technical but approachable typography
- visual proof over decorative noise
- restrained motion that emphasizes before/after transformation

Reference direction inferred from the brief:

- product-led landing page
- visible proof near the top
- utility-first layout where the tool is available immediately

Anti-direction inferred from the brief:

- purple-on-black AI cliché
- noisy glassmorphism
- playful consumer-app styling
- generic stock-illustration marketing page

### Design Principles

- Put the tool in the first screen. This is a task-first product, not a story-first brand site.
- Prove every major claim visually. “Local”, “lossless”, and “fast” should each be supported by UI evidence, not just labels.
- Treat privacy as a design feature. The interface should repeatedly reinforce that images stay on-device.
- Keep keyword-rich copy readable. SEO terms should live inside helpful content blocks, not look pasted into the design.
- Prefer mobile clarity over decorative density. Large touch targets, clear upload affordances, and obvious status states come first.

### Assumptions To Confirm

- launch language is English-first
- light mode is the primary experience
- the site should feel more technical and trustworthy than playful or consumer-lifestyle
- no strict brand color has been mandated yet
